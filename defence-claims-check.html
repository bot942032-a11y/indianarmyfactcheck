    Three habits that stop most hoaxes

    Pause

    Alarming messages are built to be forwarded within seconds. Notice the urge, then wait before you act on it.

    Trace

    Find who posted it first and whether a notice, a transcript or a full recording exists. A screenshot of a screenshot is not a source.

    Cross-check

    Look for the same claim on the organisation's own website and in other outlets before you decide it is true.

    Method · this is the sample organisation's process

    How we verify

    Every check follows the same five stages, and every verdict has a published meaning. Replace this process with your own when you adapt the site.

    Intake

    Claims reach us from readers, from the submit form and from our own monitoring of public posts. Each claim gets a case number and a saved copy: a screenshot and the link, with the date and time we captured it, so the claim cannot change under us.

    We log where it appeared, who posted it first as far as we can tell, and how far it has spread.

    Output: case file with captured copy

    Triage

    We take claims that are specific, checkable and harmful if believed: false alerts, forged notices, doctored images, clipped quotes. We skip opinions, jokes and predictions, because no evidence can settle them.

    Claims that could put people at immediate risk, such as a false road closure or a false evacuation order, move to the front of the queue.

    Output: accept or decline, with the reason recorded

    Evidence

    We look for the primary source: the notice, the full recording, the statement, the dataset. For images and video we run reverse searches, read metadata, compare frames and check location, weather and shadows against the stated date.

    We ask the organisation concerned for comment and record whether it replied and when. Every source is saved with a date.

    Output: evidence trail a reader could repeat

    Second review

    An editor who did not do the check reads the evidence trail and tries to break the conclusion. They look for a missing source, a leap in logic and a verdict that claims more than the evidence supports.

    If the two cannot agree on a verdict, the check is rated Unproven or sent back for more evidence.

    Output: verdict approved or returned

    Publish and correct

    We publish the claim, the verdict, the evidence and the sources. Each check shows when it was last updated. Corrections appear at the bottom of the check and are never made silently.

    Unproven checks are reopened when new evidence appears.

    Output: published check with update history

    What each verdict means

    ✕ False

    The evidence contradicts the claim. A reliable source or a direct record shows the opposite.

    ~ Misleading

    Something true has been cropped, relabelled or framed so that people draw a wrong conclusion.

    ✓ Verified

    Independent evidence confirms the claim, usually a primary source we could check ourselves.

    ? Unproven

    There is not enough evidence to confirm or reject the claim. We say what is missing.

    What we do not do

    • We do not rate opinions, satire or predictions.
    • We do not publish unit locations or details that could put people at risk.
    • We do not speculate about who is behind a claim without evidence.

    Corrections

    If we get something wrong, we change the check, add a dated correction note at the bottom and change the verdict if the evidence requires it. Report an error from the About page.

      What makes a claim checkable

      • It states something specific: a place, a date, a number.
      • It can be compared with a record, a recording or a notice.
      • It is going around now, so a check helps.

      Before you submit

      Do not include personal details of private people, and do not share anything that could put someone at risk.

      About and contact

      Independent by design

      Defence Claims Check belongs to no army, ministry, party or government. It is a sample you can study and adapt.

      What this site is

      A working example of a multi-page fact-checking site: a searchable archive with verdict filters, a published method, a message analyser, a quiz and a claim form.

      What it is not

      • It is not a real fact-checker. No verdict here applies to a real event, person or organisation.
      • It does not use any official name, emblem or uniform imagery.
      • It does not receive submissions. The form never leaves your browser.

      Making it yours

      • Rename the site and replace the sample checks with your own sourced work.
      • Write your own method, verdict definitions and corrections policy.
      • Publish under your own name and domain. If you represent an official body, publish from that body's own verified domain so readers can trust where it comes from.
